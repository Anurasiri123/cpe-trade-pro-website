Order block signals drawn straight on the chart

The Smart Money Concepts engine in CPE Trade Pro finds order blocks, fair value gaps, resting liquidity and structure breaks from the candles themselves — then labels each one, including the entry zone behind the current signal.

7 min read Updated 2026 Category Signals

An order block is only useful if you can actually see it in the right place. CPE Trade Pro runs a Smart Money Concepts engine over the candles and draws every detected level straight onto the chart, so the analysis sits on the same screen as the signal instead of on a separate worksheet you build by hand.

The engine starts from raw price. It reads the swing highs and swing lows, works out the current market structure, and then marks the zones that mattered: the order blocks and fair value gaps left behind by strong moves, the resting liquidity sitting above equal highs or below equal lows, and the structure breaks that changed the story.

Every element is labelled. Order blocks show as OB, fair value gaps as FVG, resting liquidity as LIQ. Structure breaks are drawn as BOS ↑ and BOS ↓ when price breaks a swing point with the trend, and as CHoCH ↑ and CHoCH ↓ when price breaks one against it.

A level is only drawn when the engine can place it in absolute candle indices. That means a zone does not drift or float around — it sits on the exact candles that created it, so what you see lines up with the chart in front of you while you scroll back through history.

Broken order blocks are filtered out. Once price has closed through a zone, that zone has either already done its job or failed, so the engine drops it from the active set and keeps your chart free of levels that no longer matter.

A last reminder before the detail: a zone is context, not an order. An order block on the chart does not place a trade by itself. It tells you where a reaction is more likely. Entries only happen when the signal conditions line up and you choose to act.

How a zone feeds the signal

Once the zones are placed, the entry logic uses them. A bullish signal looks for a demand order block or a bullish fair value gap underneath price; a bearish signal looks for the mirror image above it. The zone becomes the entry area, and the signal is tagged with the exact zone it came from — for example LIMIT ENTRY · OB or MARKET ENTRY · FVG.

This is why the label matters. It lets you see the reason behind a signal at a glance. If the tag points to a zone you do not trust, you can skip the setup. If it points to a zone that lines up with your own read of structure, you have one more reason to take it seriously.

Swing points, structure and the broken-zone filter

Market structure is the backbone of the whole overlay. A swing high is a candle high with lower highs on both sides of it; a swing low is a candle low with higher lows on both sides. Line the swings up and the engine knows whether structure is bullish, bearish or ranging.

That read is what separates a BOS from a CHoCH. A break with the trend confirms continuation; a break against it warns of a possible reversal. Both are drawn as level lines so you can see exactly which swing point gave way.

The same swing logic drives the broken-zone filter. When price closes through an order block, that block is marked broken and removed. You are then left with the zones that are still intact — and, more usefully, with the zones that are still close to current price, which are the ones a trader can actually act on.

Keeping the chart readable

Dozens of overlapping boxes help nobody. On smaller screens, only the labels that fit are drawn, so the chart stays legible on a phone as well as on a desktop. The engine draws the strongest, most relevant levels and leaves the noise out, which keeps the focus on the zones the signal is built from.

FAQ

Does an order block signal place a trade automatically?

No. A drawn zone is analysis, not an order. Trades are only opened in LIVE mode with your own connected platform credentials, or followed as paper trades in DEMO.

What is the difference between BOS and CHoCH?

BOS means price broke a swing point in the direction of the trend, which points to continuation. CHoCH means price broke a swing point against the trend, which warns of a possible reversal.

Why are some order blocks missing from the chart?

Broken order blocks are filtered out. Once price closes through a zone, it is removed so the chart only shows levels that are still intact.
