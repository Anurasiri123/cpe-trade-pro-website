How to connect Binance

Connecting is done entirely on Binance's side, with a key you create and control. A sensible setup looks like this:

  1. Log in to your own Binance account and open API Management.
  2. Create a new API key and give it a clear label, for example “CPE Trade Pro”.
  3. Keep the secret key private. Binance shows it only once, so copy it somewhere safe.
  4. Limit the permissions: enable what the app needs (spot trading) and leave withdrawals disabled. Never tick permissions you do not use.
  5. If you use an IP allow-list on Binance, add the address your app connects from.
  6. Paste the key and secret into the app's connection screen and test the connection.
  7. Keep the app in DEMO until you are comfortable, then switch to LIVE when you are ready.
🔐
Your keys, your account The API key is created by you and stays tied to your Binance account. The app uses it to read your balances and, in LIVE mode, to place market orders. It is not a custody arrangement, and nothing is shared with a third party. Limit the key's permissions and revoke it any time from Binance.

The signal and the reason, on one chart

Binance signals — quick answers

Does the app hold my funds or my API secret?

No. You create the API key yourself inside your own Binance account, and it stays tied to that account. The app uses it to read data and, in LIVE mode, to place market orders. There is no custody of your funds and nothing is shared with a third party.

Can the app short on Binance Spot?

No. Spot is long-only in the app. Short positions require Binance USDT perpetual futures or a supported Forex workflow, and leverage there can magnify losses.

How accurate are the signals?

The app's signal engine grades and scores every setup on a 0-10 scale, and keeps its recorded outcome. Past signal results do not guarantee future performance.

Do I need to connect Binance to use the app?

No. You can run everything in DEMO mode with no exchange connection. LIVE is only needed when you want the app to place real market orders.
