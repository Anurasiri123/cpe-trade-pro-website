How CPE Trade Pro analyses a chart

No black box. Here is exactly what the app reads, how a signal is built step by step, what the 0-10 quality score actually measures, and where the entry, stop and targets come from.

11 min read Updated 2026 Category How it works

Most signal tools show you a badge and nothing else: BUY. You are left to guess why, where to enter, where it is wrong and where to get out. CPE Trade Pro is built the other way round — the analysis comes first, and every conclusion is readable on the chart.

This page walks through the whole chain: candles in, decision out.

1. From candles to a decision

At the smallest level, a chart is just a list of candles — open, high, low, close and volume for each time period. The engine's job is to turn that list into a structured conclusion: a direction, a place to enter, a place where the idea is proven wrong, targets to aim at, and a score that says how clean the setup is.

It does this for one pair on one timeframe at a time. Change the pair or the timeframe and the analysis runs again from scratch — which is why the same coin can read BUY on the 4-hour chart and HOLD on the 15-minute chart. Both answers are correct for their own chart.

2. What a finished signal contains

A direction on its own is not tradeable. A complete CPE Trade Pro signal carries the direction, four prices and a score:

3. BUY, SELL and HOLD — the only three answers

Every pair and timeframe ends in one of three states. There is no "maybe", because a maybe is what makes people hesitate and then chase.

DirectionMeaningWhere it applies
BUYA qualified bullish setupSpot and Futures
SELLA qualified bearish setupFutures and Forex, where shorting is possible. On Spot it reads as "avoid or exit longs"
HOLDNo qualified setup right nowEverywhere. It means wait — do not force a trade

HOLD is not an error and it is not the app "having no idea". It appears when the conditions genuinely do not line up: structure is unclear, momentum contradicts the trend, price is stuck mid-range, or there is no sensible place to put a stop. Those are exactly the conditions where most losses are made, so the app says nothing instead of saying something wrong.

4. The 0-10 quality score, part by part

Once a direction qualifies, the app does not stop. It grades the setup with a 0-10 quality score built from five measurable parts, each worth up to 2 points. Every part reads a different kind of evidence, so a high score means several independent checks agreed — not one indicator shouting loudly.

PartWhat the app is askingReads from
ClarityIs there a clean pattern agreeing with the direction?Chart patterns, candlestick patterns
TrendDo structure, the higher timeframe and trend strength point the same way?Market structure, higher-timeframe bias, ADX, Smart Money Concepts bias
MomentumAre the oscillators confirming instead of fighting?RSI, MACD histogram, Stochastic crosses, RSI and MACD divergence
VolumeIs the move backed by real participation?Volume spike versus average, Money Flow Index, recent change
LevelsIs this a sensible place to enter, in a calm enough market?Bollinger band edges, Parabolic SAR, support and resistance map, volatility level
ℹ️
Why five parts and not one score A single number hides its reasoning. Five parts let you see instantly why a setup is strong or weak — for example a 7.5 built on Trend and Levels is a different trade from a 7.5 built on Momentum alone.

5. Grades: A+, A, B, C and D

The raw 0-10 score is also bucketed into a letter grade, which is simply easier to scan across a watchlist than a decimal.

The app also shows a plain tier label alongside the plan — CAUTION, STRONG and HIGHER STRONG — so you can filter a long list quickly. A higher grade means more of the checklist was satisfied. It does not mean a trade cannot lose.

6. How the analysis happens automatically

You are not drawing anything. The app picks the pair and timeframe, pulls the candles, runs the studies and lands on the decision. Seen from the outside it looks like one motion: a scan across the chart, a focus on the region that matters, then the result.

7. Where the entry, stop and targets come from

The levels are not round numbers pulled from a hat. They are taken from the chart's own structure:

  • Entry — placed at the zone the setup is based on: a Bollinger band, a Fibonacci level, an order block or a fair value gap. The app labels it MARKET ENTRY if it is taken at price, or LIMIT ENTRY if it waits at a zone.
  • Stop loss — placed beyond the level that would prove the idea wrong, usually just past the swing or the Smart Money Concepts invalidation point. A minimum gap is enforced so the stop is never unrealistically tight.
  • Target 1 — the first logical level in front of price: the middle or opposite band, or a nearby Fibonacci level.
  • Target 2 — the further objective, such as the opposite band or a Fibonacci extension.

Every level is drawn on the chart with its label, so the plan is visible rather than hidden behind a button. That also means you can look at the distance between entry and stop, and between entry and targets, and judge whether the trade is worth taking before you click anything.

⚠️
A stop is not optional A signal without a defined invalidation point is not a signal. The app always computes one — use it, and never widen it after entering.

9. What the score is not

Be precise about this, because the words matter:

  • It is not a win rate. It does not know the outcome of the trade.
  • It is not a probability of profit and it is not "accuracy".
  • It is not an audited track record. It grades the setup in front of it, nothing more.

What it is: a transparent summary of how many independent checks agreed, so you can compare setups and be more selective. The decision, the position size and the risk stay with you.

10. How to use it — a simple routine

  1. Start with DEMO so you can learn the app's language without risking money.
  2. Pick a market you understand — a handful of crypto pairs, or a couple of Forex pairs. Not the whole list.
  3. Work one or two timeframes. Higher timeframes give cleaner structure; lower ones give more noise.
  4. Read the five parts, not just the total. A high score with weak Volume tells you something specific.
  5. Check the plan before the direction. If the stop is too far for your risk per trade, the trade is too big — not "too good to miss".
  6. Prefer HOLD to forcing. Waiting for a qualified setup is the skill that survives longest.
  7. Journal the outcome in the app's signal history, then review what you actually did — not what the app said.
