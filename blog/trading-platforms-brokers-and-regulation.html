Trading platforms, brokers and regulation: an honest guide

Where do you actually trade crypto and Forex, who are the brokers, who regulates them, which countries say no, and how does money actually come back out? Here is the plain-English version — with CPE Trade Pro's place in the picture made clear.

11 min read Updated 2026 Category Industry

When people say "my trading platform", they usually mean three different things at once: the software on the screen, the company that holds the account, and the tool that tells them what to do. Those are separate businesses with separate jobs, and mixing them up is how beginners get hurt. This page sorts all three out, names the well-known players, explains who watches them, which countries restrict trading, and exactly how a withdrawal is supposed to work.

⚠️
Rules differ by country and change often This guide is general education, not legal or financial advice. Regulation, tax and availability differ by country and change frequently. Before you open any account, check the current rules on the regulator's own website and read the exchange's or broker's terms for your country.

2. Who the crypto exchanges are

Crypto is traded on exchanges rather than through banks. The largest international ones you will hear named include Binance, Coinbase, Kraken, OKX, Bybit, KuCoin, Bitget, Gate.io and MEXC. Some focus on spot, some are best known for derivatives, and their availability changes from country to country — very often a global site and a separate, licensed local version exist side by side.

🏦 Centralised exchange A company runs the order book and holds accounts. Fast and easy — but you are trusting a business with your funds.
🔑 Withdraw early, small Deposit a little, withdraw a little, and see it arrive before you trust the platform with more.
🌍 Country version The same brand can be licensed differently in different countries. Always use the version that serves your country.

3. Who the Forex brokers are

Forex is reached through a broker — a licensed firm that gives you access to the currency market. Well-known international names you will come across include IC Markets, Pepperstone, XM, Exness, OANDA, IG, FXCM, FBS and HotForex. Brokers differ in spreads, execution, minimum deposit, leverage and — most importantly — which regulator licenses them.

One broker name can hold several licences at once, in different jurisdictions, each with different client protections and leverage limits. The version of the firm that serves your country is the one you must check, because that is the one whose rules apply to you.

💡
Compare the licence, not the advertisement A broker's marketing page often shows the most impressive licence it holds anywhere in the world. What matters is the licence attached to the entity that will actually hold your account.

4. Who regulates them

Financial firms are watched by national regulators, and a licence is the firm's permission to operate. These are the bodies you will see named most often.

5. Which countries restrict trading

"Available" and "restricted" are not permanent labels — they shift with policy. Some well-known examples:

🚫 China — crypto trading and exchanges banned 🇺🇸 USA — separate local versions, lower leverage 🇬🇧 UK / EU — retail leverage capped 🇯🇵 Japan — very tight FX leverage 🇮🇳 India — legal but heavily taxed 🇱🇰 Sri Lanka — not legal tender; verify rules
  • China has banned crypto trading and exchanges for residents — a hard restriction rather than a tax.
  • The United States often requires a separate, locally licensed version of a global exchange, and its Forex rules cap retail leverage far lower than offshore brokers offer.
  • UK and EU regulators cap the leverage a retail client may use, usually around 1:30 on major currency pairs.
  • Japan is stricter still, with a very low retail FX leverage cap and licensed exchanges only.
  • India treats crypto as legal but taxes it heavily, with a transaction tax on top — profit is possible, keeping it is the hard part.
  • Sri Lanka does not recognise crypto as legal tender, and the Central Bank has publicly warned about the risks of virtual currencies. Rules here can change, so verify with official sources before you act.
⚠️
Availability is not permission A website being reachable from your country does not mean it may legally serve you. If you use a platform that is not licensed for your country, you may have no protection and no route to complain if something goes wrong.

6. How withdrawals actually work

You can only withdraw from the broker or exchange that holds your money — never from an analysis app, because an analysis app never holds it. A genuine withdrawal follows a predictable path.

Three practical habits make withdrawals boring, which is exactly what you want:

  • Test early. Deposit a small amount, withdraw a small amount, and confirm it arrives — before you trust the platform with more.
  • Finish your KYC first. Identity checks are normal and required. Being unverified is the most common reason a withdrawal stalls.
  • Expect normal fees, not gatekeeping fees. Network fees on crypto and bank fees on fiat are normal. A fee whose only purpose is to unlock your withdrawal is not.

7. How to protect yourself

Most harm in retail trading comes not from bad signals but from bad counterparties. The warning signs are consistent and worth memorising.

🚫 Guaranteed profits "Risk-free", "guaranteed returns" or a promised monthly percentage. Real markets do not offer guarantees.
💸 Pay-to-withdraw Any request for a fee or tax before releasing your money. This is the classic exit scam.
🧑‍💼 An "account manager" Someone who trades for you, often after a chat-group introduction, and then cannot be reached.
🕵️ No register entry A licence number that does not appear on the regulator's own public register.
  • Check the licence number on the regulator's website, never only on the firm's own page.
  • Never send money to a personal bank account or personal crypto wallet.
  • Be suspicious of profits shown only as screenshots — images prove nothing.
  • Prefer platforms that let you start in a demo or practice mode before any real money moves.
