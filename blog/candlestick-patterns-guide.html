Candlestick patterns: every candle tells a story

A candlestick shows the open, high, low and close of one period in a single picture. The body tells you who won the bar; the wicks tell you where they failed. Learn the language of candles and you can read what the market is feeling, bar by bar.

14 min read Updated 2026 Category Technical analysis

Every candlestick pattern is a small story. The body (the thick part) shows the distance between open and close — green means buyers won the bar, red means sellers won. The wicks (thin lines above and below) show the extremes the market reached but could not hold. A long wick is a rejection: someone pushed price there and got pushed straight back.

💡
Context is everything The same shape means different things at different places. A Hammer at the bottom of a drop is bullish; the same shape at the top of a rise (a Hanging Man) is a warning. Always read the candle with its location.

1. Indecision candles

These candles show that neither buyers nor sellers could take control. They are not signals on their own — they are a warning that the market is pausing and a big move may follow.

Doji

Open and close are almost equal — a tiny body or a cross. The market opened, fought, and ended exactly where it started. That is indecision.

  • Bias: Neutral — wait for the next candle
  • Best at: after a strong move, or near a Bollinger Band extreme
  • Rule: do not FOMO a Doji. Wait for confirmation.

Long-Legged Doji

Very long upper and lower wicks with a tiny body. A violent two-way fight that ended where it began. High volatility, but zero certainty about direction. A big move often follows — wait for the break candle.

Dragonfly Doji

Open = close = high, with a long lower wick. Sellers pushed price down and buyers slammed it straight back up. Strongest at support or the lower Bollinger Band after a drop.

  • Bias: BUY
  • Stop: under the wick tail

Gravestone Doji

Open = close = low, with a long upper wick. Buyers pushed up and sellers dragged it back to the low. Strongest at resistance or the upper Bollinger Band. A spike high that closes at the low is a failed breakout.

  • Bias: SELL

Spinning Top

A small body with upper and lower wicks of similar length. Both sides fought and nobody won clearly. Often appears inside a Bollinger Band squeeze. Pause trading and wait for the break.

2. Single-candle reversals

One candle that signals the trend may be tiring. Best at support or resistance, and stronger with indicator confluence (RSI, Bollinger Bands, Fibonacci).

Hammer

A small body at the top, with a long lower wick (about twice the body) and little or no upper wick. After a drop, this means buyers rejected lower prices and stepped back in.

  • Bias: BUY
  • Stop: under the wick low
  • Best with: lower Bollinger Band + RSI rising from oversold
  • Psychology: wait for the wick to finish — never buy mid-wick

Shooting Star

A small body at the bottom, with a long upper wick after a rise. Sellers rejected the highs.

  • Bias: SELL
  • Stop: above the wick high
  • Best with: upper Bollinger Band / Fibonacci extension / RSI overbought

Inverted Hammer

Like a Shooting Star but at a bottom — a long upper wick with a small body. Weaker than a Hammer; use it as an alert, not an entry. Wait for the next green candle to confirm.

Hanging Man

Looks exactly like a Hammer but appears after a rise. The shape is the same, but the context makes it a possible top exhaustion. Confirm with the next candle and RSI before reversing.

3. Engulfing patterns

An engulfing candle's body completely covers the previous candle's body. It is a one-bar takeover — the second candle's side dominated completely.

Bullish Engulfing

A green body that fully covers the prior red body. Buyers took control in one bar.

  • Bias: BUY
  • Best at: support, lower Bollinger Band, Fibonacci 50–61.8%
  • Stop: under the engulfing low
  • Money rule: a wide engulfing body means a wide stop — shrink your size

Bearish Engulfing

A red body that fully covers the prior green body. Sellers dominated.

  • Bias: SELL
  • Best at: upper Bollinger Band + RSI above 70
  • If already long: take partial profit or trail the stop instead of hoping

Outside Bar

The second candle engulfs the first from high to low — not just the body, but the whole range. A violent takeover inside one bar. Trade in the direction of the outside bar's close. A big bar means a big stop, so size smaller.

4. Multi-candle reversals

These patterns take two or three candles to complete. They tell a fuller story than a single candle and are usually more reliable.

Morning Star

Three candles: a big red, a small "star" candle (gap down, small body), then a big green that closes well into the first red's body. A bottom reversal story.

  • Bias: BUY
  • Entry: after the third candle closes
  • Stop: under the star low
  • Confluence: lower Bollinger Band + bullish RSI divergence

Evening Star

The mirror: a big green, a small star gap up, then a big red closing into the first green's body. A top reversal.

  • Bias: SELL
  • Rule: do not short the middle star alone — wait for the third close

Piercing Line

A red candle, then a green that opens lower but closes above the midpoint of the prior red. Softer than an engulfing — confirm with an RSI turn.

  • Bias: BUY

Dark Cloud Cover

A green candle, then a red that opens higher but closes below the midpoint of the prior green. An early warning before a full bearish engulfing. Take partials on longs.

  • Bias: SELL

Bullish & Bearish Harami

A big candle, then a small candle whose body sits inside the prior body. The big move is losing steam.

  • Bullish Harami: big red then small green — selling pressure shrinking. Needs confirmation.
  • Bearish Harami: big green then small red — buying pressure shrinking. Take partials on longs.

Three Inside Up / Down

A Harami plus a third candle that closes beyond the first. The inside bar finally broke. Far stronger than a bare Harami.

5. Continuation candles

These candles show the trend is still in control. They are not reversals — they are reasons to stay with the move (or join a pullback).

Marubozu

A long body with almost no wicks. One side controlled the entire bar from open to close.

  • Bull Marubozu: strong BUY pressure — trend continuation
  • Bear Marubozu: strong SELL pressure — do not catch the falling knife
  • Risk: chasing a late Marubozu without a pullback to the middle band or Fibonacci level

Three White Soldiers

Three rising green candles with higher closes. Steady, committed buying.

  • Bias: BUY trend
  • Entry: pullbacks to Fibonacci 38–50% or the middle Bollinger Band
  • Warning: overconfidence after three greens — keep your risk percentage fixed

Three Black Crows

Three falling red candles with lower closes. Steady selling. Do not average down — wait for structure. RSI oversold can stay oversold.

Rising / Falling Three Methods

A long candle, three small candles that stay inside its range, then another long candle in the same direction. The trend rested without breaking. The small candles are noise, not reversal.

6. Tweezer patterns

Two candles with matching highs or matching lows. The market tested the same level twice and got rejected. Tweezers are the small-scale cousins of Double Tops and Bottoms.

Tweezer Top

Two candles with matching highs. Resistance tested twice. Mark the high as structure; a break above it invalidates the pattern.

  • Bias: SELL
  • Confluence: RSI failure at the highs

Tweezer Bottom

Two candles with matching lows. Support tested twice.

  • Bias: BUY
  • Stop: just under the matching low

7. Gap & kicker patterns

Gaps appear more often in stocks and forex than crypto, but the logic applies everywhere: the market repriced while you were away.

Bullish & Bearish Kicker

A candle, then a gap into an opposite-colour candle with no overlap. The mood flipped between closes.

  • Bullish Kicker: red then gap-up green — strong BUY
  • Bearish Kicker: green then gap-down red — exit longs fast

Abandoned Baby

A red candle, a gapped-down doji, then a gapped-up green. The doji is a lonely island of indecision. Rare but high quality at bottoms. The gaps on both sides are required.

Breakaway Gap

A gap out of a range or base. Unlike an exhaustion gap, it appears early in a move. The gap often acts as support or resistance later.

8. How to use candles without getting chopped

  • Location first. The same candle means different things at a top versus a bottom. Draw support and resistance before reading candles.
  • Confirm with indicators. A Hammer at the lower Bollinger Band with RSI turning up is far stronger than a Hammer in the middle of nowhere.
  • Wait for the close. A candle is not complete until the period closes. Mid-bar wicks lie.
  • Size from the stop. The wick or pattern tells you where the stop goes. A longer wick means a wider stop — shrink your position size.
  • Never trade a single candle alone. A candle is a clue, not a verdict. Combine it with structure, indicators and the higher-timeframe trend.
