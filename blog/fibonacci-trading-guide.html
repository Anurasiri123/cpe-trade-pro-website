Fibonacci Explained: Levels, Retracement, Extension and Targets

Fibonacci levels are one of the most requested chart tools and one of the most misunderstood. Here is what retracement and extension actually measure, how to draw them honestly, and where CPE Trade Pro reads them as part of its level analysis.

10 min read Updated 2026 Category Learn

Type "Fibonacci" into any chart and you get a fan of horizontal lines that look precise, almost scientific. That precision is exactly why the tool is misused. The levels are not laws of the market — they are a structured way to mark where a pullback might pause, and they only earn their keep when something else on the chart agrees with them.

This article covers what the levels are, how to draw them, the golden pocket, how extensions set targets, and how the CPE Trade Pro analysis reads Fibonacci as part of its score.

What Fibonacci is (and is not)

The ratios come from a number sequence in which each figure is the sum of the two before it: 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21, 34... Divide one number by the next and you approach 0.618. Divide by the number two places ahead and you approach 0.382. Those two numbers, plus their simple relatives, produce the retracement levels traders use every day: 23.6, 38.2, 50, 61.8 and 78.6 percent.

What Fibonacci is: a way to divide the distance of a move into proportional zones so you can talk about where price is, not just which direction it is heading. What it is not: a prediction, a signal on its own, or a guarantee that price will reverse. The 50 percent level is not even a Fibonacci number — it is simply the midpoint, included because markets often pause halfway.

ℹ️
Fib is about location, not direction A Fibonacci level tells you a price is interesting. It does not tell you the trend will resume. That judgment comes from structure, momentum and context — which is why CPE Trade Pro scores Fibonacci as one input, never the whole answer.

The retracement levels (figure)

A retracement measures how far a pullback has travelled against the previous move. A 61.8 percent retracement means price has given back roughly 61.8 percent of the last swing. The figure below shows a swing from low to high, with the standard levels drawn across it.

How to draw a retracement

Fibonacci is only as good as the swing you attach it to, and the swing is a choice. A simple, repeatable method:

  1. Find a clean impulse. Look for a move that clearly left a low and reached a high (or the reverse) without chopping sideways in the middle.
  2. Anchor wick to wick. Connect the lowest wick to the highest wick of that swing. Use the same convention every time so your zones are comparable.
  3. Check the higher timeframe first. A fib on the daily swing outranks a fib on the 15-minute. If they overlap, the region is stronger.
  4. Leave it alone. Redraw only when a genuinely new swing forms. Moving the tool to fit price is how the levels stop meaning anything.

The golden pocket 61.8–65%

The single most watched zone is the cluster between 61.8 and 65 percent — often called the golden pocket. It is deep enough to look like a real pullback, shallow enough that the trend can still be intact, and it is where the 0.618 ratio overlaps with the 0.65 reading many traders add.

Two honest caveats. First, price trades through the pocket all the time — a level is a zone, not a wall. Second, the pocket is only interesting when it lines up with something else: a moving average, a prior support shelf, a volume shelf, or an order block. On its own it is a band on a chart.

Different levels tend to matter in different contexts:

23.6% shallow, strong trend 38.2% common pullback 50% midpoint 61.8% golden pocket 78.6% deep, last defence

Extensions and setting targets

If retracements measure a pullback, extensions project beyond the original swing to estimate where a move might travel next. The common extension ratios are 1.272 and 1.618, with 2.0 as a further objective. Traders use them to place take-profit levels instead of guessing at round numbers.

⚠️
A level is a zone, not a wall Price can slice straight through 61.8% without a pause. Never risk more simply because price reached an "important" ratio — Fibonacci tells you the price is interesting, not that it will turn.

Common mistakes

  • Drawing on every wiggle. If the swing is not a clear impulse, the levels are noise.
  • Ignoring the higher timeframe. A 15-minute fib inside a daily downtrend is fighting the tide.
  • Using Fibonacci alone. A level without structure, momentum or volume behind it is just a line.
  • Redrawing to fit price. Moving the anchors after price moves is curve-fitting your own chart.
  • Treating 61.8% as a guarantee. It is watched a lot, which is precisely why it sometimes gets swept before the real move.

How CPE Trade Pro uses Fibonacci

In the app, Fibonacci is one input among several, not a standalone signal. When a pullback is measured, Fibonacci levels feed the Levels part of the 0-10 quality score — the same part that reads Bollinger band edges, support and resistance and volatility. That part is worth up to 2 points. Moving averages and ichimoku-type trend studies, by comparison, feed the Trend part.

The score itself is built from five measurable parts — Clarity, Trend, Momentum, Volume and Levels — each capped at 2 points, for a maximum of 10. Fibonacci contributes to Levels only. It is worth being exact about what that means: the 0-10 score is not a win rate, not a probability of profit and not an audited performance record. It simply reports how much of the checklist agreed.

Practically, a well-aligned golden pocket that also sits on a moving average and a prior support shelf is more likely to lift the Levels part of the score than a level floating alone in open space. That is the honest role of Fibonacci here: it makes a location a little more or less interesting, and the score reflects that.
