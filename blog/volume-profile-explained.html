Volume Profile Explained: POC, Value Area and Key Levels

Most volume tells you when trades happened. A volume profile tells you where. Here is the POC, the value area (VAH/VAL), HVN and LVN, and how to use them.

11 min read Updated 2026 Category Learn

Ask most traders what volume is and they point to the bars along the bottom of the chart — volume by time, one bar per period. A volume profile asks a quieter, more revealing question: where did that volume actually trade, price by price? The answer is a horizontal histogram stuck to the side of the chart, and it changes how you see support and resistance.

This guide covers the volume profile and ordinary volume, the point of control (POC), the value area (VAH/VAL), high- and low-volume nodes, naked and composite profiles, and how to use all of it alongside VWAP. Educational content only — nothing here is financial advice.

Volume profile vs ordinary volume

Ordinary volume answers "how much traded in this 15-minute bar?" A volume profile answers "how much traded at this price, across the whole window?" Same data, different axis. Time-based volume tells you if activity is picking up; price-based volume tells you where the market agreed and where it disagreed.

That shift sounds small but it is not. Prices where lots of volume changed hands are prices traders care about, and they tend to act as magnets or barriers later. Thin prices, where the market rushed through, tend to be travelled quickly again. You are no longer guessing where support "should" be — you are reading where the market actually did its business.

💡
Profiles are drawn from real fills A volume profile is built from actual traded volume at each price, not from orders waiting. That makes it a record of agreement rather than intention — which is what makes its levels worth watching.

POC (point of control)

The point of control (POC) is the single price with the most volume in the profile. It is the price the market, collectively, chose most often — the fairest price in the window. Because so much business happened there, the POC often acts as a magnet: price drifts back to it, and it can be a natural reference for balance and rotation.

The POC is not magic, and it is not a trade signal on its own. Its value is contextual: a POC in the middle of a range usually means price is balanced; a POC far below current price may mark a level that fewer participants ever traded above, which can make that area sensitive.

Value area (VAH/VAL)

The value area is the price range that contains roughly 70% of the traded volume in the profile. Its upper edge is the VAH (value area high) and its lower edge is the VAL (value area low). Together they describe the band the market treated as fair value for that period; the POC sits inside it by definition.

In practice the edges do the work. Price accepted above the VAH or below the VAL is often read as the start of a new trend or a breakout, while price rotating back inside the band suggests balance. Because so many traders watch the same two lines, the VAH and VAL frequently behave like horizontal support and resistance.

High- and low-volume nodes (HVN/LVN)

Within a profile, volume bunches into high-volume nodes (HVNs) and thins into low-volume nodes (LVNs). An HVN is a price shelf where heavy trade built up; it tends to attract price and slow it down, acting like a magnet or a soft barrier. An LVN is a price the market skipped through; because few orders were ever filled there, price often moves through again quickly.

This is where volume profile earns its keep. Traders use HVNs as areas to expect congestion or reaction, and LVNs as likely "fast lanes" where a breakout can run. A move that breaks out of an HVN and reaches an LVN above it may accelerate simply because there is little volume to slow it.

Naked POC and composite profiles

A naked POC (sometimes called an untested or virgin POC) is a point of control from a previous session or swing that price has not since returned to. Traders watch these because the market never "resolved" that price — the heavy agreement happened once and was never revisited. Many treat a naked POC as a potential magnet that price may drift back toward.

Composite profiles do the same job over a longer window, stacking many sessions into one profile so you can see where volume accumulated over weeks rather than a day. A composite HVN shows a genuinely significant level; a composite LVN shows a wide zone the market has repeatedly skipped. The longer the window, the more meaningful the level — and the slower it tends to move.

Using it with VWAP and support/resistance

Volume profile works best as part of a small set of complementary references, not in isolation. Pair it with VWAP: VWAP gives you the day's average traded price, while the POC and value area give you the day's most-agreed price range. When VWAP sits near the POC, the market is balanced; when price trades well above both, it is stretched.

Layer classical support and resistance on top. Where a horizontal level from old highs or lows lines up with a VAH, a VAL or a naked POC, you have confluence — several independent reasons for price to react in one place. A profile level on its own is one opinion; a profile level stacked with a chart level and VWAP is a stronger case. ⚠️
Levels are areas, not exact lines A volume profile is built from a chosen window and a chosen tick size, so its levels shift a little every time you change either. Treat the POC, VAH and VAL as zones of interest, and always combine them with price action rather than trading them blindly.
