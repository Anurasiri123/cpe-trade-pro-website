Signal types in CPE Trade Pro — and how to trade them

The app speaks in a small, fixed vocabulary: BUY, SELL and HOLD, each in a spot and a futures version, each carrying a grade and a 0-10 quality score. Here is what every one of them means — and a calm routine for acting on it.

12 min read Updated 2026 Category Signals

A "signal type" is not a special kind of magic. It is simply the shape the analysis takes when it is finished. Different markets and different accounts can only do certain things — a spot wallet can buy but cannot short; a perpetual futures account can do both — so the app has to be clear about which kind of signal you are looking at.

This page covers all of them, in order: the three directions, the spot and futures versions, the grades and the 0-10 score, the four prices that make a signal tradeable, and then a plain routine for each. If you want the engine itself explained, start with how CPE Trade Pro analyses a chart.

The three directions

Every pair, on every timeframe, ends in exactly one of three states. There is no "maybe", because a maybe is what makes people hesitate and then chase. The direction tells you which side of the market the setup favours — not how much to risk, and not that the trade will work.

DirectionMeaningWhere it applies
BUYA qualified bullish setupSpot and Futures
SELLA qualified bearish setupFutures and Forex, where shorting is possible. On Spot it reads as "avoid or exit longs"
HOLDNo qualified setup right nowEverywhere. It means wait — do not force a trade

HOLD is not an error and it is not the app "having no idea". It appears when the conditions genuinely do not line up: structure is unclear, momentum contradicts the trend, price is stuck mid-range, or there is no sensible place to put a stop. Those are exactly the conditions where most losses are made, so the app says nothing instead of saying something wrong.

BUY, SELL and HOLD

On screen the three directions look like three cards, each with its own colour, its own meaning and its own score. Seeing them side by side is the quickest way to understand that they are different answers to the same question — "is there a qualified setup here?" — rather than three shades of the same trade.

Spot vs futures versions of a signal

The same analysis runs across both market types, but the action differs because the instrument differs. On spot you can only buy and hold, so a BUY is a long idea and a SELL is a warning to avoid or exit — not an instruction to short. On futures (USDT perpetuals) both directions are usable, so a BUY and a SELL each produce a full plan with entry, stop and two targets.

Forex and metals behave like futures in this respect: pairs such as XAUUSD can be sold as easily as bought, so SELL signals are fully tradeable there too.

🪙 Spot signal Long-only. BUY builds a position; SELL means "avoid or exit longs". No shorting, no leverage forced on you.
⚡ Futures signal Both directions are tradeable on perpetuals. BUY and SELL each carry entry, stop, target 1 and target 2.
💱 Forex & metals Two-sided by nature. The same engine reads XAUUSD and FX pairs through OANDA, FXCM and the MT4/MT5 bridges.

Grades and the 0-10 score

Once a direction qualifies, the app does not stop. It grades the setup with a 0-10 quality score built from five measurable parts, each worth up to 2 points: Clarity, Trend, Momentum, Volume and Levels. A high score means several independent checks agreed — not one indicator shouting loudly. The raw number is then bucketed into a letter grade, which is simply easier to scan across a watchlist.

ScoreGradePlain English
8.0 - 10A+Almost every check agreed
6.5 - 7.99AMost checks agreed
5.0 - 6.49BA workable setup with some gaps
3.5 - 4.99CThin evidence — be selective
Below 3.5DLittle agreement. Usually best skipped
ℹ️
What the score is not The 0-10 score is not a win rate, not a probability of profit and not an audited performance record. It is a transparent summary of how many independent checks agreed, so you can compare setups and be more selective.

A signal is four prices, not a badge

A direction on its own is not tradeable. What makes a CPE Trade Pro signal usable is that it always arrives as a complete plan: an entry, a stop loss and two targets. Those four prices are the signal. The direction and score tell you why it is interesting; the prices tell you what to actually do.

How to trade a BUY

A BUY is a qualified bullish setup. Acting on it is mostly about patience and arithmetic, not conviction:

  1. Check the plan, not the colour. Look at the distance from entry to stop. If that distance is wider than your risk per trade allows, the trade is too big — skip it or reduce size, never widen the stop.
  2. Decide your entry style. The app labels it MARKET ENTRY when the trade is taken at price, or LIMIT ENTRY when it waits at a zone. A limit entry is calmer and gives a better price, but it may not fill.
  3. Set the stop before you enter, at the level the app drew. That is your defined risk.
  4. Plan the exits. Target 1 is the nearer level where partial profit is often taken; target 2 is the further objective for the rest.
  5. Leave it alone. Moving the stop wider after entering is the single most common way a small loss becomes a large one.

How to trade a SELL

A SELL is a qualified bearish setup, and it only becomes a trade where shorting is possible — futures or Forex. On spot, treat it as guidance to avoid or exit longs rather than an instruction to short.

  • Confirm the market type first. A SELL on a spot-only pair is information, not a position.
  • Same rules as a BUY, mirrored. Entry above price, stop above the invalidation level, targets below.
  • Be more selective in uptrends. A counter-trend short is a harder trade than a short that agrees with structure.
  • Respect the score. A SELL with a low score and weak Trend is usually best left alone.
💡
Direction is the start, not the decision The app gives you a plan you can read. Your job is to check whether the risk in that plan fits your account — that is what turns a signal into a trade you can live with.

What to do with HOLD

HOLD is the most useful type and the hardest to obey. It is the app telling you that no setup qualified: structure is unclear, momentum disagrees with trend, price is stuck, or there is no sensible place for a stop. In those conditions, doing nothing is the correct action.

Most beginner losses come from treating HOLD as an invitation to browse for a different indicator that will say "buy". It will. There is always a chart somewhere that agrees with what you already want to do. The discipline is to accept HOLD as a real answer and move on.

⚠️
HOLD is a decision, not a delay Waiting is not wasted time. It is the position that keeps your capital intact for the setup that actually qualifies.

A 60-second pre-trade checklist

Before any order, run this in your head. If a line fails, the trade fails — there is always another setup.

  1. Direction clear? BUY or SELL, not a forced HOLD.
  2. Market type matches? A SELL needs futures or Forex, not spot.
  3. Score sensible? You can read the five parts, not just the total.
  4. Stop defined? And within your risk per trade.
  5. Position size set? From risk, not from feeling.
  6. Targets noted? Where you take partial profit and where the rest exits.
  7. Plan written? So you review what you did, not what you remember.
