Chart types explained: candles, Heikin Ashi, bars, area and line

The same market can be drawn five ways. Each chart type hides some detail and reveals something else — and knowing which one to reach for is a genuinely useful skill. Here is what each type in CPE Trade Pro is best for.

11 min read Updated 2026 Category Charts

Every chart type is built from the same four numbers per period: the open, the high, the low and the close — usually shortened to OHLC. What changes between chart types is how much of that information is drawn, and how much is smoothed away.

Candles show everything. Heikin Ashi averages it. Bars show everything in a different shape. Area and line keep only the close, which makes long-term trends clean but hides the intraday fight. None of them is "correct". They are lenses, and a good trader switches deliberately rather than by habit.

Why chart type matters

Chart type changes what your eye notices first. A noisy candle chart makes you think about every wiggle; a smoothed Heikin Ashi chart makes the same price action look like a calm trend. Neither is a lie — but if you do not know which one you are looking at, you can be fooled into reading smoothness as certainty.

There is one rule worth stating plainly: the app's analysis always runs on standard candlestick data. The other four styles change how the chart is drawn for you, not what the engine reads. That keeps a signal consistent no matter which view is on screen.

💡
One habit, one benefit Use candles for the plan — entries, stops and targets need real prices. Use a smoothed view to check whether the bigger trend still agrees with you. Switch to a clean line when you only care about direction.

Candlestick charts

The candlestick is the default for a reason: it carries the most information in the least space. The thick body spans open to close, and the thin wick (or shadow) marks the high and low. A green body means the period closed above its open; a red body means it closed below.

Heikin Ashi

Heikin Ashi ("average bar") is a smoothed chart. Each bar is calculated partly from the previous one, which takes the edge off choppy price action. The result is longer runs of same-coloured candles and far fewer false flips.

Two things to know before you use it: the Heikin Ashi open and close are not the real market prices, and the high and low can be shifted. So it is excellent for reading trend and momentum at a glance — long runs of green suggest a healthy uptrend, small bodies and frequent colour changes suggest hesitation — but it is a poor place to read exact entry and stop prices. Use it to confirm direction, then place the plan on a candle chart.

Bar (OHLC) charts

A bar chart shows the same OHLC data as a candle, drawn differently: one vertical line for the high-to-low range, a tick to the left for the open and a tick to the right for the close. Nothing is hidden, and nothing is smoothed.

Area charts

An area chart keeps only the close and shades everything underneath it, so the series reads as a filled shape rather than a line. The gradient gives the eye a sense of "weight" and makes long, dragging moves feel smoother and easier to follow.

Area charts are for the big question — is this thing broadly going up or down? — not for fine detail. There are no wicks, so the exact high and low are invisible, and there is nothing to read a candle pattern from. Use it when you zoom out.

Line charts

A line chart is the sparest option: a single stroke joining each close. It strips away every bit of noise and shows just the shape of the trend. When a chart feels too busy and you cannot tell whether you are in an uptrend or a sideways chop, a line chart answers that in a second.

Which type for which job

There is no ranking, only fit. This table is the short version of everything above:

Chart typeShowsBest for
CandlestickFull OHLC, bodies and wicksPatterns, entries, stops and anything that needs exact prices
Heikin AshiSmoothed average barsReading trend and momentum without noise
Bar (OHLC)Full OHLC as ticks and a lineDetailed price reading on dense, low timeframes
AreaClose only, with a filled bodyZoomed-out trend, a calm sense of direction
LineClose onlyThe fastest possible read on trend direction
ℹ️
Mix, do not marry Most experienced traders keep candles as their working view and borrow the others for specific questions. Switching type is a tool, not a personality.
