BOS vs CHoCH: structure break or change of character?

Two labels sit at the heart of reading market structure: a Break of Structure and a Change of Character. One is a continuation signal, the other is a warning. Confusing them is one of the most common mistakes in Smart Money Concepts trading.

9 min read Updated 2026 Category Technical analysis

Before you can find an order block or a fair value gap worth trading, you need to know which way the market is going. That judgment comes from market structure, and the two events that describe it are the Break of Structure (BOS) and the Change of Character (CHoCH). They look similar on a chart and are easy to mix up, but they mean different things.

💡
Short version BOS = price breaks a swing point with the trend → continuation. CHoCH = price breaks a swing point against the trend → possible reversal. A CHoCH is a warning, not a confirmation.

1. Swing points and how structure is defined

Structure is built from swing points:

  • A swing high is a candle high with lower highs on both sides of it.
  • A swing low is a candle low with higher lows on both sides of it.

Line those swings up and you get a sequence. If each high is above the previous high and each low is above the previous low, structure is bullish (higher highs, higher lows). If each high is below the previous high and each low is below the previous low, structure is bearish. When the swings neither rise nor fall consistently, the market is ranging — and range conditions are exactly where structure signals tend to be misleading.

Every BOS and CHoCH is defined relative to these swings, so the first skill is simply learning to mark them cleanly.

2. Break of Structure — continuation

A Break of Structure (BOS) happens when price closes beyond the most recent swing point in the direction of the current trend.

  • In an uptrend, a BOS is price closing above the previous swing high.
  • In a downtrend, a BOS is price closing below the previous swing low.

A BOS is a continuation signal. It says the trend just confirmed itself again. Traders usually respond by looking for the next pullback into a demand or supply zone, rather than buying or selling the breakout candle itself — because chasing the break often means entering at the worst price.

3. Change of Character — possible reversal

A Change of Character (CHoCH) is the opposite event: price breaks a swing point against the current trend.

  • In an uptrend, a CHoCH is price failing to make a new high and instead closing below the last meaningful swing low.
  • In a downtrend, a CHoCH is price failing to make a new low and instead closing above the last meaningful swing high.

CHoCH is an early reversal warning, not a guaranteed turning point. It tells you the character of the market may be changing, so long setups (or short setups) now need extra evidence before you trust them.

5. How to label them on a chart

Labeling is simple once your swings are marked:

  1. Mark the most recent swing high and swing low.
  2. Wait for price to close beyond one of them — not just wick through it.
  3. Ask which direction that break points relative to the trend.
  4. If the break is with the trend, label it BOS. If it is against the trend, label it CHoCH.
  5. Add an arrow to show the direction: up for a bullish break, down for a bearish break.
  6. Update the trend assumption, then repeat with the next swings.

Consistency matters more than perfection. If you mark swings the same way every time, your BOS and CHoCH labels will mean the same thing from chart to chart.

6. Why CHoCH alone is not a reversal confirmation

This is the part beginners get wrong. A CHoCH only tells you that a structure point failed — it does not prove the market has reversed. In a strong uptrend, price can dip below a minor swing low, trigger a CHoCH, and then rip straight back to new highs. Traders call that a failed CHoCH, and it traps anyone who sold the first break.

To treat a CHoCH as a real reversal, look for extra evidence:

  • Follow-through. Price makes a lower high after the CHoCH instead of reclaiming the level.
  • A clean supply zone. The bounce that creates the lower high comes from a real order block, not a random candle.
  • Higher-timeframe agreement. The higher timeframe is also losing its structure.
  • Momentum shifting. Indicators like RSI or MACD start to disagree with the old trend.

Without at least some of these, a CHoCH is best treated as “slow down”, not “flip direction”.

⚠️
One CHoCH is not a trend Structure changes often need more than one break to confirm. The first CHoCH warns you; the following structure is what confirms the new direction.

7. Combining BOS / CHoCH with order blocks

Structure tells you the direction; order blocks tell you where to act. The two work best together:

  • BOS then pullback. After a BOS confirms the trend, wait for price to return to the order block that caused the move. The BOS gives you direction, the block gives you a location.
  • CHoCH then new zone. After a CHoCH, look for the order block that produced the reversal leg. That zone is your candidate area for the first trade in the new direction.
  • Rejection of a zone. If price returns to an old zone and blows straight through it, that is extra confirmation that structure has changed.

In both cases the sequence is the same: structure first, zone second, entry third, risk always.

8. False breaks and how to filter them

Not every break is real. A candle can poke beyond a swing point and close back inside it — a wick, not a break. To filter weak signals:

  • Use candle closes, not wicks. A break that closes beyond the level is far more reliable than one that only wicks through it.
  • Respect the higher timeframe. A tiny break on the 1-minute chart is noise if the daily chart disagrees.
  • Wait for the retest. Price that breaks and then holds the level on the retest is showing real intent.
  • Watch volume and momentum if you have them. A break with no follow-through is suspect.
  • Beware obvious levels. A high or low that everyone can see is a magnet for stop hunts — sometimes it is more about liquidity than structure.

10. Summary

A Break of Structure and a Change of Character describe two different things. BOS is price continuing in the direction of the trend; CHoCH is price breaking against it and warning that the trend may be ending. Learn to mark swings consistently, label breaks by their direction relative to the trend, and treat every CHoCH as a question rather than an answer. Combine structure with order blocks and strict risk control, and you have a workable way to read any chart.
