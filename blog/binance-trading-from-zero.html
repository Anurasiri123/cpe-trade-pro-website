Binance from zero: a simple, safer start

New to Binance? Follow the steps in order: secure your account, learn Spot, understand orders and decide your risk before placing anything. The diagrams make the first choices easier to see.

9 min readUpdated 2026Category Beginner

Binance is a cryptocurrency exchange: people use it to buy and sell digital assets. The interface has many tools, but a beginner does not need to use them all. Start with account security and a small Spot trade workflow. Futures and leverage can wait until you understand the risks.

1. Create and secure your account

Use Binance's official website or app for your country. Follow its identity checks and local rules. Availability, payment methods and product access differ by location, so check Binance's current terms before depositing.

Before adding funds, set up an authenticator or passkey where available, use a unique password, enable anti-phishing protections, and save backup codes offline. Never share your password, one-time codes, recovery phrase or API secret with anyone who contacts you.

⚠️
Protect API keysIf you connect analysis software, create keys only from the official Binance account page. Grant only the permissions you need; keep withdrawals disabled. Never paste a secret into chat, screenshots or a public file.

2. Start with Spot, not leverage

In Spot, you buy or sell the asset itself using the available balance in your account. A Futures position is a contract and can use leverage; losses can grow quickly and liquidation is possible. A beginner can learn the exchange layout in Spot without adding leverage.

3. Understand a pair such as BTC/USDT

A pair has a base asset and a quote asset. In BTC/USDT, BTC is the asset being priced and USDT is the unit used to show its price. If the display says 60,000, it means one BTC is quoted at about 60,000 USDT at that moment. The exchange price moves constantly.

Check the exact pair, available balance, minimum order size and estimated fees before confirming. Similar-looking tokens can have different names and risks.

4. Know the order you are placing

⚡MarketRequests a quick fill at available prices. The final price can differ in a fast market.
🎯LimitSets the price you are willing to accept. It may not fill if the market never reaches it.
🛡️StopTriggers an order after a chosen price is reached. Trigger and fill prices can differ.

5. Decide the maximum loss before the trade

Pick a small amount you can afford to lose and calculate the position from the distance to your exit level. One simple planning formula is position size = planned maximum loss ÷ stop-distance percentage. Fees and slippage can make the actual result worse than the estimate.

For example, a hypothetical $1,000 account risking 1% sets a maximum planned loss of $10. If the planned stop is 2% from entry, the notional position would be about $500 before fees. This is an arithmetic example, not a recommendation. A stop does not guarantee an exact fill or cap every loss during a gap or outage.

6. Practise, then review

Use a paper-trading or demo mode to practise finding a pair, choosing an order and recording an exit. Binance product names and demo availability can change. CPE Trade Pro's DEMO mode is a separate paper simulation; it does not place an order on Binance.

After each practice trade, note why you entered, where your planned exit was, what fees applied and whether you followed your rules. Avoid borrowing money, chasing a fast price move or increasing size to recover a loss.

7. Connect an analysis app carefully

If you choose to connect CPE Trade Pro, use the official Binance API settings and the app's DEMO mode first. For LIVE access, create a dedicated API key with only the required trading permissions, keep withdrawals disabled, and follow the app's connection guide. Test with the smallest practical amount only after you understand every order setting.
