Forex trading from zero: a simple start

Currency pairs, pips and lots, spreads and leverage, sessions — and how to connect a broker to CPE Trade Pro so the app can analyse the charts for you. A beginner's path, in plain English.

11 min read Updated 2026 Category Beginner

Forex has a reputation for being complicated. It is not, once you accept one idea: you are always trading two currencies against each other, and everything else — pips, lots, spreads, leverage — is just vocabulary for measuring that one relationship. This page walks through the basics in order, then shows how to connect a broker so CPE Trade Pro can read the charts.

1. What you actually trade in Forex

In Forex you trade currency pairs. Buying EUR/USD means buying euros while selling dollars, so you profit if the euro strengthens against the dollar. You are not taking delivery of banknotes; you are taking a position on the exchange rate between two currencies.

The first currency in a pair is the base, the second is the quote. A price of 1.0842 on EUR/USD means one euro buys 1.0842 US dollars. When that number rises, the euro is gaining; when it falls, the euro is losing ground. Every Forex trade is that same idea, just with different currencies.

2. Pairs: majors, minors and exotics

Pairs are grouped by how heavily they trade, which usually matches how tight their spreads are.

💱 Majors Always include the US dollar — EUR/USD, GBP/USD, USD/JPY. The most liquid and tightest typical spreads.
🔗 Minors Majors without the dollar — EUR/GBP, EUR/JPY. Still liquid, slightly wider spreads.
🌍 Exotics A major paired with a smaller economy's currency. Thinner liquidity and much wider spreads.
🥇 Metals Pairs such as XAUUSD, traded on many Forex platforms alongside currencies.

A beginner is usually best served by a handful of majors. Fewer pairs means fewer decisions and more attention on each chart.

3. Pips, lots and position size

A pip is the standard unit of movement. For most pairs it is the fourth decimal place — 0.0001. If EUR/USD moves from 1.0842 to 1.0843, it moved one pip. (For yen pairs the pip is the second decimal, because those prices are quoted differently.)

A lot is how much you are trading, and it decides what one pip is worth to you. A standard lot is 100,000 units, a mini lot is 10,000, and a micro lot is 1,000. The smaller the lot, the smaller the money value of each pip — and the smaller your risk.

Position size follows the same logic as any market: decide the maximum you are willing to lose, then size the lot so that the distance to your stop equals that amount. A stop placed further away needs a smaller lot; the risk stays the same.

4. Spread, leverage and margin

The spread is the difference between the price at which you can buy and the price at which you can sell. It is the built-in cost of every trade: you begin slightly behind, and the market has to move past the spread before you are in profit. Tighter spreads (majors, busy sessions) are cheaper; wider spreads (exotics, quiet hours) are dearer.

Leverage lets you control a large position with a small deposit. It exists because currency moves are small, so without leverage the profits would also be small — but leverage magnifies losses in exactly the same way. The deposit you put up is your margin, and if a move uses up too much of it, the broker can close your position automatically. That is the Forex version of a forced exit.

⚠️
Leverage is not extra money Leverage increases the size of your position, not the size of your account. A 20x position can be wiped out by roughly a 5% move against it — on a market that moves gently, that is still entirely possible.

5. Sessions and the best times

Forex trades around the clock from Monday to Friday, but activity comes in waves as the world's financial centres open. Tokyo, London and New York take turns, and each brings a character of its own. London is typically the largest session; New York adds the US data releases.

The liveliest window is usually the London and New York overlap, when two of the biggest centres are open at once. Spreads tend to be tightest and moves sharpest then. Late in the New York session and through the Asian morning, charts often drift with far less to do.

💡
Pick one window and master it You do not need to trade all day. Choosing a single session that fits your schedule — and learning how the market behaves inside it — is far more useful than watching every hour.

8. Common beginner mistakes

✕ Too much leverage ✕ Trading quiet hours ✕ Ignoring the spread ✕ Too many pairs at once ✕ Moving the stop

Two of these deserve a word. Wide spreads in quiet hours or on exotic pairs quietly raise the bar every trade must clear — sometimes profitably, sometimes fatally. And leverage is the classic beginner trap: the position looks small because the deposit is small, until a gentle move does real damage.

ℹ️
Fewer pairs, better focus A short watchlist of majors is easier to learn than a long list of exotics. Depth beats breadth when you are starting out — and it is easier to notice when a setup is genuinely clean.
