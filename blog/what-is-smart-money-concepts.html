What are Smart Money Concepts (SMC)?

Order blocks, fair value gaps, liquidity and structure breaks — the ideas behind SMC, explained without jargon. Plus: how CPE Trade Pro labels each one directly on your chart.

8 min read Updated 2026 Category Technical analysis

Smart Money Concepts (usually shortened to SMC) is a way of reading a chart that focuses on where large orders are likely sitting, instead of only on classic indicators. It grew out of price-action trading and institutional order-flow theory, and it looks at four things over and over:

  1. Market structure — is the market making higher highs and higher lows, or lower highs and lower lows?
  2. Order blocks — the last candle before a strong move, where big orders were likely filled.
  3. Fair value gaps — a price gap left behind by a fast move, which price often comes back to.
  4. Liquidity — obvious high and low levels where stop orders cluster.
💡
Short version SMC does not predict the future. It tells you where a reaction is more likely, so you can plan an entry, a stop and a target around that zone — instead of chasing price.

1. Market structure comes first

Before any zone matters, you need to know the direction. SMC describes structure using swing points:

  • A swing high is a candle high with lower highs on both sides of it.
  • A swing low is a candle low with higher lows on both sides of it.

Line the swings up and you get a sequence. If each high is above the last high and each low is above the last low, structure is bullish. Flip it, and structure is bearish. When neither applies, the market is ranging — and range conditions are exactly when many signals should be ignored.

2. BOS — Break of Structure

A Break of Structure (BOS) happens when price closes beyond the most recent swing point in the direction of the trend. In an uptrend, that means price pushes above the previous swing high.

A BOS is a continuation signal. It says: the existing trend just confirmed itself again. Traders usually respond by looking for the next pullback into a demand zone, rather than buying the breakout candle itself.

3. CHoCH — Change of Character

A Change of Character (CHoCH) is the opposite event: price breaks a swing point against the current trend. In an uptrend, price fails to make a new high and instead closes below the last meaningful swing low.

CHoCH is an early reversal warning, not a guaranteed turning point. It tells you the character of the market has changed and that long setups now need extra evidence.

BOSA swing point with the trendTrend continuation
CHoCHA swing point against the trendPossible reversal

4. Order blocks

An order block (OB) is the last opposite-coloured candle (or small cluster of candles) immediately before an impulsive move that breaks structure. The idea is simple: that is the price area where a large buy or sell order most likely entered, and price often returns to it before continuing.

  • Bullish order block — the last down candle before a strong up move. Usually treated as a demand zone.
  • Bearish order block — the last up candle before a strong down move. Usually treated as a supply zone.

An order block stops being interesting once price has already closed through it — traders call that a broken order block. A useful filter is to ignore broken zones and only work with the ones that are still intact and reasonably close to current price.

⚠️
Not every block is a zone An order block only means something if it caused a real displacement — a fast, wide move that broke structure. A random candle with a big body is not automatically a zone.

5. Fair value gaps

A fair value gap (FVG) — sometimes called an imbalance — is a three-candle pattern where the wicks of candle 1 and candle 3 do not overlap.

  • Bullish FVG: the low of candle 3 is completely above the high of candle 1. The empty space between them is the gap.
  • Bearish FVG: the high of candle 3 is completely below the low of candle 1.

A gap like this means price moved so quickly that one side of the market barely traded. Many traders expect price to revisit part of that gap later — the market “filling in” the imbalance.

6. Liquidity

Liquidity in SMC terms means clusters of pending orders, and the easiest place to find them is right above equal highs or right below equal lows. Those are where breakout traders put stops and where stop-loss orders pile up.

When price spikes through such a level and then quickly reverses, traders describe it as a liquidity grab or stop hunt. The level itself is drawn as a dotted guide line, not as a trade signal.

7. Where SMC fits with normal indicators

SMC is not a replacement for indicators — it is a context layer. A practical stack looks like this:

🏗️ Structure Decide the trend with swings, BOS and CHoCH on a higher timeframe.
📦 Zone Find the order block or fair value gap you would accept a trade from.
📈 Timing Use RSI, MACD or Bollinger Bands plus a candle confirmation to time the entry.
🛡️ Risk Place the stop beyond the zone, size the position, define target 1 and target 2.

That is the whole idea: structure → zone → timing → risk. Skipping a step is where most SMC trades fall apart.

9. Common beginner mistakes

  • Trading every zone. Most order blocks never get touched. Filter them by trend and by distance from price.
  • Ignoring the higher timeframe. A perfect bullish OB on 5 minutes means little inside a daily downtrend.
  • Entering with no invalidation. If you cannot say where the idea is wrong, you cannot size the trade.
  • Confusing a gap with a signal. An FVG is a location. The entry still needs confirmation.
  • Revenge trading after a stop. One zone, one plan, one stop.

10. Summary

Smart Money Concepts gives you a vocabulary for structure and location: swings, BOS, CHoCH, order blocks, fair value gaps and liquidity. Used as context alongside indicators and strict risk planning, it helps you stop chasing price and start planning entries. Used as a magic prediction tool, it will cost you money.
