Spot, futures, manual and auto: how to trade in CPE Trade Pro

What you own, which direction you can take, and how you act on a signal — by hand, half-way, or automatically. Here is every mode in CPE Trade Pro, and why DEMO comes before LIVE.

10 min read Updated 2026 Category Modes

A signal is only half of trading. The other half is deciding where that signal lives — on spot or futures — and how you turn it into an action: manually, with a confirmation, or automatically. This page lays out each choice in plain language so you can pick a mode that matches your experience and your temperament.

1. What is spot trading

In spot trading you buy and sell the actual asset. Buy one coin on spot and you own one coin; it sits in your account until you sell it, send it or hold it. There is no expiry date and no contract to roll over, so the idea is as simple as buying something and later selling it.

Because you own the asset, spot is naturally long-only: you buy first, then sell higher. You cannot "go short" on spot in the straightforward sense, because shorting would mean selling an asset you do not actually hold. There is no funding to track and, on a plain spot position, no liquidation.

2. What is futures trading

A futures contract is an agreement tied to the price of an asset. In crypto, the most popular form is the perpetual contract — a futures contract with no expiry that keeps tracking the spot price, usually settled in a stablecoin such as USDT.

Futures differs from spot in two ways. You can go long or short, so a falling market is tradeable. And you can use leverage, posting a smaller amount of margin to control a larger position. Leverage brings two ideas spot never has: funding rates, a small ongoing payment between longs and shorts, and liquidation, where the exchange closes your position if a move uses up your margin.

💡
Short version Spot = you own the coin, long-only, no expiry. Futures = you trade a contract, can go long or short, usually with leverage — which also brings funding and liquidation.

3. Long only vs long and short

Long means you profit when price rises; short means you profit when price falls. Spot supports only the long side, because a short would require selling an asset you do not hold. Futures supports both.

CPE Trade Pro follows how the markets really work. On the Spot side the app is deliberately long-only, so a spot BUY is the natural trade. When a setup is bearish, the app does not pretend you can short a spot asset — instead that idea belongs to Futures (or a supported Forex workflow), where shorting genuinely exists. This keeps every signal honest: the direction you see always matches a trade you can actually place on that market. For a deeper comparison, see spot vs futures crypto trading.

4. Acting on a signal manually

Manual is the most direct mode. The app produces the plan — a direction with an entry, a stop loss and two targets, plus a 0-10 quality score — and you place the order on your own platform, using your own judgement about size and timing.

  • Full control. You decide whether to take the trade, and at what price.
  • Full responsibility. Nothing happens unless you click, so your habits matter most.
  • Best for learning. Placing each order yourself is how the language of entries and stops becomes second nature.

5. Semi-auto: alerts and confirmations

Semi-auto sits between manual and automatic. The app watches the market and, when a qualified setup appears, it alerts you — but it still waits for your confirmation before anything is done. You stay in the loop, and you keep a veto.

This is often the most comfortable middle ground: you are not glued to a screen waiting for a setup, yet you are not handing the decision over entirely. Think of it as the app saying "this is worth your attention" and leaving the final word to you.

6. Auto trading in the app

In auto mode the app acts on qualified signals using the risk settings you have saved — on your own connected account, with your own API keys. You choose the markets, the size and the limits; the automation handles the repetition, so it does not depend on you being awake or disciplined in the moment.

Two things to be clear about. First, auto trading runs on your account: the app is analysis software, not a broker, and it never holds your funds or your keys. You can start and stop it at any time. Second, automation removes hesitation — it does not remove risk. A losing trade is still a losing trade, and leverage still magnifies losses. Auto means consistent execution, not guaranteed results.

ℹ️
Automation is not a promise Auto mode helps you act consistently on the signals you would otherwise miss or second-guess. It does not raise the odds of any single trade, and it cannot remove the risk that a trade loses. Treat it as a discipline tool, not a guarantee.

8. Which mode should you start with

Start simple and only add complexity when the simpler version feels easy.

1️⃣ DEMO + manual Learn the app, place every order yourself, and read each plan at no real cost.
2️⃣ DEMO + semi-auto Let alerts find qualified setups, confirm each one by hand, and build a routine.
3️⃣ LIVE + manual, tiny size Same rules, real money, smallest size that still feels real.
4️⃣ LIVE + auto Only once the routine has held up live — and never straight after a lucky run.

Many traders never leave step two or three, and there is nothing wrong with that. Auto is a convenience for a settled process, not a shortcut to one.

⚠️
Do not auto-trade a strategy you have not tested Automation repeats whatever you give it, including mistakes — and it does so faster and more often than you would by hand. Prove the routine in DEMO first.
