Chart tools and signals

Bollinger Bands (BB)

Learn what the middle, upper and lower bands show; how volatility squeeze and expansion work; and why touching an outer band is not automatically a reversal. Combine location with market structure and a candle close. Read the full Bollinger Bands guide.

RSI (Relative Strength Index)

Study momentum, common overbought/oversold readings, and divergence. RSI can stay elevated or depressed in a strong trend, so use it as context rather than a standalone buy/sell switch. The RSI and MACD guide explains a combined checklist.

Fibonacci (Retracement & Extension)

Measure a clear swing from low to high (or high to low) and study common retracement areas such as 38.2%, 50% and 61.8%. Extensions can help map possible target zones, but levels are references, not guaranteed turning points. Prefer a confluence with structure or a candle confirmation.

CPE Signals — meaning and what to do

Understand BUY, SELL and HOLD, and review the entry, stop, target and five-factor 0–10 quality score. The score summarizes chart, trend, momentum, volume and levels evidence; it is not a win rate or probability of profit. Read what a complete trading signal contains.

Chart Lab Indicators: Stoch, ADX, Ichimoku and VWAP

Explore additional tools available in Chart Lab: Stochastic for momentum context, ADX for trend strength, Ichimoku for trend/support-resistance structure, and VWAP as a volume-weighted reference. Their readings can conflict; learn each tool separately and test combinations on DEMO.

Elliott Wave (light count)

Learn the basic idea of impulse and corrective swings and use a light wave count as one possible chart interpretation. Wave counts are subjective and can be redrawn as price changes. Keep an invalidation level and do not treat a count as a forecast.

3-Month Forecast (Forecast 3M)

Learn how the app's optional Forecast 3M projection is displayed in future chart space. It is a model-based visual scenario, not a guaranteed price path, a fixed target or financial advice. Compare it with current structure and remember that uncertainty grows with forecast horizon.

Risk, decisions and review

Money Management

Plan trade size from the distance to the stop and a pre-set amount of account risk. If the stop must be wider, the position size should generally be smaller. Never choose size from excitement or from a score alone.

Psychology for Trading

Build routines that help you follow a written plan: patience, discipline, and accepting that a valid setup can still lose. Record whether you followed the process separately from the financial outcome.

FOMO and Emotional Discipline

Recognize chasing after a fast move, revenge trading, and fear of missing out. A missed trade is not a loss. Use a checklist and let an unconfirmed setup pass rather than entering because price is moving quickly.

Risk & Money Management — deep

Go further into risk per idea, exposure across correlated positions, losing streaks and a maximum loss limit. Decide these boundaries before a session. A risk plan limits damage; it cannot remove market risk.

Stop Loss & Take Profit mastery

Place a stop at a level that invalidates the idea, then size the trade to fit the stop distance. Plan partial exits or targets before entry. Stops can slip in fast or thin markets, and a target is not assured.

Trade Plan & Journaling

Write down the market, timeframe, setup, entry reason, invalidation, target plan and emotional state. Review a meaningful set of trades for repeatable process errors instead of changing a method after one result.

Practice Routine & Backtesting

Choose one setup, define its rules in advance, and review a consistent sample of historical examples. Include fees and realistic execution assumptions where possible. Then practise in DEMO before considering LIVE activity. A backtest is not a guarantee of future results.

Markets and platform lessons

Forex signals — meaning and what to do

Learn how to read supported Forex pair and metal setups, and why spread, session liquidity, broker data and contract sizing matter. Confirm that the selected broker connection supports the instrument and order type you intend to use.

Leverage, Margin & Liquidation

Understand that leverage magnifies exposure relative to the funds posted as margin. Adverse moves can trigger liquidation, and losses can happen quickly. Learn the mechanics before using leverage; compare Spot and Futures trading and practise in DEMO.

Volatility & Martingale

Study how volatility changes stop distance, position size and execution. The module also explains martingale-style size escalation as a high-risk method: increasing size after losses can rapidly compound drawdown and does not guarantee recovery. Do not treat it as a safe recovery plan.
